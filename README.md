Verifying Property Credentials

One of the most significant mistakes first-time buyers make is neglecting to verify the property's credentials. Ensure that the flat you are interested in has clear titles and is free from legal disputes. Here are some critical documents to check:


    Title deed to confirm the seller's ownership.
    Encumbrance certificate to ensure no liabilities exist on the property.
    Approved building plans from the local authorities.
    Occupancy certificate to ensure that the flat complies with local regulations.
    Previous sale agreements to establish a transparent transaction history.
    Tax receipts proving that the property taxes have been paid up to date.

Inspecting the Property Thoroughly

Another critical mistake is failing to conduct a comprehensive inspection of the property. You should thoroughly evaluate the condition of the flat, including:


    Structural integrity: Look for cracks, dampness, and other signs of wear.
    Electrical systems: Check outlets, wiring, and circuit breakers.
    Plumbing: Inspect for leaks, water pressure, and overall plumbing health.
    Ventilation and air quality: Ensure adequate airflow and absence of mold.
    Furnishings: Assess the quality and condition of any included fixtures.
    Safety features: Verify smoke detectors, fire alarms, and security systems.


Conducting a proper inspection can reveal hidden issues that may affect the flat's value or your living conditions.
